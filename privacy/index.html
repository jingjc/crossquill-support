CrossQuill — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-14　Last updated: 2026-11-14

This policy explains how the App's developer (the seller shown on its App Store page) ("we") handles information in the CrossQuill apps for Mac and iPhone (the "App"). The App is a client: you use your own API keys and connect directly from your device to the AI model providers you choose. We do not operate any server that receives your chats, keys or usage data.

1. What we do not collect

  • The App has no sign-up and no developer account system.
  • The App contains no advertising, ad tracking, third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDKs, does not use the advertising identifier, and does not track you across apps or websites.
  • Your chats, attachments, system prompts, API keys and usage records are never sent to us.
  • The App writes a small number of diagnostic events to the device's system log (event names, counts and error categories for sync, saving and key access). They contain no message text, titles, keys, paths or identifiers and are not uploaded to us.

Database master key:

  • Mac: a random 256-bit master key is held by the macOS keychain. The item is not synced via iCloud Keychain and is read without an authentication prompt. If the keychain is unavailable the App stops opening data; it never falls back to plaintext, switches keys or rebuilds an existing database. The Mac App has no separate app password; everyday access relies on your macOS login session.
  • iPhone: the master key is protected by a 6-digit passcode you set (wrapped with a key derived via PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256). You may enable Face ID / Touch ID, in which case the master key is also kept in a this-device-only keychain item protected by your current biometrics; it does not move with iCloud or backups. You may also create a recovery code in advance for resetting a forgotten passcode. If you have no recovery code set up and biometrics are unavailable, we cannot recover your encrypted data.

3. Information sent to the model providers you choose

Recipients: the providers you select and for which you have saved a key. Services in this version: OpenRouter, DeepSeek, Kimi, OpenAI and Anthropic. The services available in your version are those listed under "Add service" in the App.

  • Kimi recipients differ by site: the Kimi open platform has an international site and a China site, with separate accounts and keys. The App identifies which site your key belongs to (see section 4) and then communicates only with that site. The two sites are different recipients with their own privacy policies: the international site is the Kimi open platform international site (Moonshot AI, API host api.moonshot.ai, privacy policy https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/agreement/userprivacy ); the China site is the Kimi open platform China site (Moonshot AI, API host api.moonshot.cn, privacy policy https://platform.kimi.com/docs/agreement/userprivacy ).

When data is sent:

  • First-time consent per provider: before a receiving service first gets any of your content, the App explains the recipient, the categories of data that will be sent, that provider's privacy policy link and that the provider bills you, and asks for your explicit consent. Without it the App sends none of your content and does not read your key to send content (except for Kimi site identification: until you have agreed to the current Kimi notice, the App explains it separately and asks you to confirm before identifying the site for a send; identification sends only your key; see section 4). Kimi asks per site: consent to one site does not cover the other, and using a key of the other site asks again. Consent is recorded on this device only and can be withdrawn per provider in Settings; after withdrawal, or when the notice changes, the App asks again before the next send. This consent is independent of "Confirm before sending" below; turning that off never skips it. Requests that carry only your key (model catalog, balance, Kimi site identification) do not need it.
  • "Confirm before sending" is on by default. While it is on, the App decrypts the selected provider's key and connects to that provider only after you review the recipient, model, context, attachments and web settings and tap confirm (except for Kimi site identification; see section 4).
  • You can turn "Confirm before sending" off in Settings. After that, tapping Send submits directly once on-device checks pass.
  • One confirmation can cover a batch of requests, such as the stages of a multi-model analysis, or the upload and later deletion of a Kimi document attachment. The confirmation sheet lists the scope of the batch.
  • The App never retries failed requests automatically, never silently switches models or providers, and never adds hidden prompts.

What is sent: your question as written, the system prompt you entered, earlier completed turns of the conversation (plus any reasoning/tool context the provider requires back), attachments you selected, data needed for web search or tools, and your API key for authentication.

  • Multi-model analysis: one model's answer text and source links are passed to the other providers you selected, within the scope shown on the confirmation sheet; raw reasoning is never forwarded across models.
  • Attachments: most services receive attachments inline in the request. Kimi document attachments are first uploaded as Kimi remote files on the site your key belongs to, and the App requests their deletion afterwards; a stop, failure or deletion timeout may leave a file with Kimi, and the App lists files without a deletion receipt so you can deal with them. The App records each remote file's upload site on device and sends deletion requests only to that site; if your current key belongs to the other site, those records are kept with a notice and can be deleted after you switch back to a key of that site.
  • Web search and official tools: when enabled, they are run by the selected provider (or the upstream provider it routes to) and may be billed separately. The App does not switch to a third-party search service.

How providers handle this data: once received, your data is handled under that provider's own privacy policy, terms and your account settings (including retention periods, whether it is used to improve models, and storage location). We cannot control or delete data a provider has received. Please read each provider's policies before use (links at the end of this page). Points directly tied to App settings:

  • OpenRouter: OpenRouter routes requests to upstream model providers. OpenRouter chats in the App default to "Do not store conversations": only routes marked zero data retention (ZDR) that disallow data collection are used, with no fallback to other routes on failure. You can choose "Allow storage, no collection" or "Allow storage and collection" for a run, and the App explains the consequences. Your OpenRouter account privacy settings also affect routing.
  • Anthropic: some models require 30-day data retention under Anthropic's rules; the App marks them "Requires 30-day data retention" in the model picker and on the confirmation sheet.
  • OpenAI: OpenAI requests from the App ask the service not to store response state (store: false); whether OpenAI keeps abuse-monitoring logs is governed by its policy.
  • Kimi: data is received by the site your key belongs to and handled under that site's privacy policy (the two sites have different policies; links above under "Recipients" and in section 16). If you enable the Kimi official tool "Cloud memory", Kimi may store content submitted through tool calls. The App has no way to view or delete that memory, and turning the switch off does not delete stored content.

4. Model catalogs, balance checks and Kimi site identification

  • When you tap "Save and fetch models" or "Fetch models again", or refresh a catalog, the App uses that provider's key to request its official model catalog (for OpenRouter, also the zero-data-retention endpoint list and the selected model's routes). These requests contain no chats, system prompts or attachments and do not trigger generation. Catalogs are never fetched automatically at launch.
  • Balances are requested only when you tap to check them and contain no chat content. OpenRouter balance requires a separate management key, encrypted separately from chat keys and used only for balance checks. Some services' balances cannot be checked in the App.
  • Kimi site identification: saving a Kimi key uses no network. After each launch or key change, before the first Kimi catalog read, Kimi balance check, Kimi remote file deletion or send to Kimi, the App uses the key to request the model catalog of the international site (api.moonshot.ai) first, and requests the China site's (api.moonshot.cn) catalog only if the international site rejects the key as unauthorized (HTTP 401/403). These requests carry only the key, contain no chats, system prompts or attachments, and do not trigger generation. A key that belongs to the China site is therefore sent to the international site first each time identification runs; if identification does not succeed, no result is kept and your next action starts again with the international site. Both sites can see your device's network address. On a network error, timeout, redirect or any other unexpected answer, identification stops without asking the other site and is not retried automatically. The result is kept in memory only, never written to the database or synced, and is redone after the key is replaced or deleted or the App restarts. Once identified, every request with that key (chat, attachment upload and deletion, catalog, balance) goes only to that site, and no failure is ever re-sent to the other site. Until you have agreed to the current Kimi notice, when identification is needed to send content to Kimi, the App first explains it and asks you to confirm. For catalog reads, balance checks and remote file deletion that you start yourself, the App explains it next to the action or in the deletion confirmation and does not ask separately.

5. Web sources and links

  • Whenever an answer with structured search sources from DeepSeek or OpenRouter is displayed (including when you open history or a synced conversation), the App automatically requests each source site's icon (first the site's root favicon, then, if needed, the site's home page and up to 3 icons or logo images it declares). Opening the source list also requests the pages to read their titles. These requests go only to public HTTPS addresses, carry no cookies or credentials, use a generic browser identifier and do not send your language or region preferences. Title requests go to the source page's own address. Icon requests do not include the source article's path or query, but an icon declared by a site's home page may be on another path or another HTTPS host, and a site's redirect can also send these requests to another HTTPS host. As with visiting any website, those sites and hosts can see your device's network address. Results are cached in memory only.
  • Addresses extracted from answer text (for example Kimi tool results) are never visited automatically; the App asks you to check the full address before opening.
  • Tapping a link in an answer asks for confirmation before the system browser opens it.

6. iCloud sync (optional)

  • Sync is off by default. The App reads and writes sync files only after you choose a folder (usually in iCloud Drive) in sync settings and confirm the connection. With automatic sync on, the App reads and writes that folder automatically, including after the Mac App launches or the iPhone App is unlocked, on returning to the foreground, when the folder's contents change, and when the network returns and offline updates are sent; the system or the file service you chose handles the upload and download.
  • Sync files are not encrypted by the App: sync copies are plain JSON files protected by your iCloud account and system data protection. Without iCloud Advanced Data Protection, they can be viewed directly in the Files app, Finder or iCloud on the web, and your App passcode does not cover them. If you choose a non-iCloud location, protection depends on that file service. The on-device database remains encrypted.
  • Synced content includes saved chat text, reasoning, system prompts, model information, usage, image thumbnails (attachment file names are replaced with generic names), conversation rename/pin/delete, and ordinary project grouping. Multi-model analyses have a sharing switch that is off by default; when on, their full text is synced.
  • Never synced: API keys, passcode and recovery code, unsent drafts, attachment originals, local folder access grants and absolute paths.
  • If your text itself contains keys or other sensitive content, the App does not detect or redact it; it is written out with enabled sync.

7. Device backups and migration

  • iPhone device backups (iCloud Backup or computer backups) may contain the App's encrypted database; the keychain item used for Face ID is this-device-only and is not backed up. After moving to a new device or restoring from backup, unlock with your passcode or recovery code.
  • On first launch, the Mac App can import an encrypted archive exported from an earlier version of the App; import happens on device and the archive contains no API keys or folder access.

8. System permissions

  • Face ID / Touch ID (iPhone, optional): used only to unlock encrypted local history; biometric data is handled by the system and is not available to us.
  • Files: only attachments and the sync folder you pick through system pickers.
  • Network: only for the provider requests (including Kimi site identification), source metadata requests and sync file access described above.
  • Background processing (iPhone): used to finish a confirmed generation after you leave the App, not to collect data.
  • Clipboard: copying keys, recovery codes or source links uses the local-only pasteboard (a copied recovery code expires after two minutes).

9. Purchases and information from Apple

The App is sold through the App Store and payment is handled by Apple; we do not receive your payment information. Depending on your device settings, Apple may provide developers with aggregated app analytics or crash reports; these are collected and processed by Apple under Apple's privacy policy. We may view those aggregated analytics and crash reports in the developer tools Apple provides, to find and fix problems. Model usage fees are charged separately by the providers you choose under your agreement with them.

10. Retention and deletion

Because your data stays on your devices and in the sync folder you choose, you control deletion:

  • Chats and analyses: delete a conversation or clear all local history in Manage history; delete multi-model analyses in analysis history. Deleting an ordinary project removes only the grouping; chats remain.
  • API keys: delete each one under API keys; the OpenRouter management key is deleted separately. Deleting or replacing a Kimi key discards the in-memory site identification.
  • Consent records: withdraw per provider in Settings; withdrawing deletes that provider's record.
  • Sync copies: deletion of shared records propagates only after a sync completes; files in the sync folder are yours to manage in the Files app or Finder. To keep offline devices from restoring deleted content, sync copies keep deletion markers (including project names).
  • Deleting the App: removes its app data; whether system keychain items and the Mac app container are also removed depends on system behavior.
  • Data already received by providers: handled under each provider's policy; contact the provider or use its console when needed (e.g. Kimi remote files, Kimi cloud memory). Kimi remote file deletions go only to the upload site; records of the other site are kept until you switch back to a key of that site, or you can handle them in that site's console.

11. Security

We use the measures described above: on-device encryption, key isolation, decrypting a key only when it is needed under the rules above and, for provider requests, redirect rejection and key-echo blocking. No system is perfectly secure; please protect your device, passcode, recovery code and iCloud account.

12. Children

The App is not directed to children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone. Providers may set their own age requirements; please check their terms.

13. Your rights

We do not hold your chats, keys or usage data, so we generally cannot access, correct or delete them on your behalf; you can manage them in the App or ask the relevant provider. If applicable law grants you other rights, contact us as below.

14. Changes

If the App's data handling changes (for example a new provider or feature), we will update this policy and its "Last updated" date; significant changes will be noted both on the support page and in the App's release notes.
